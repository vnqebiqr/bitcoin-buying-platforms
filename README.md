## How much Bitcoin should you buy?

There is no universally correct amount. A reasonable starting point is an amount you can afford to lose without affecting rent, bills, emergency savings, or high-interest debt payments.

Bitcoin can move sharply in either direction. A purchase that looks small in dollars can still experience a large percentage change. Buying more does not make the position safer; it only increases the amount exposed to the market.

Some buyers use a fixed-dollar approach:

1. Choose a budget.
2. Buy the same dollar amount on a schedule.
3. Avoid making every purchase based on a short-term price move.
4. Keep records of each transaction.

Others prefer to make one purchase and hold. Neither method removes market risk. The important point is to decide the size and timing before placing the order, rather than increasing the amount because the checkout screen makes the purchase feel easy.

## What fees should you compare?

When comparing where to buy Bitcoin, examine the full cost instead of one advertised fee.

### Trading fee

This applies mainly to order-book spot trading. Maker and taker rates can differ, and your account tier may affect the rate. The applicable fee should be checked inside the platform for the specific market.

### Spread

The spread is the difference between the price at which you can buy and the price at which you can sell at roughly the same time. With an instant Buy quote, some costs may be reflected in the all-in price rather than shown as a separate trading-fee line.

### Payment-provider cost

A bank, card network, or payment provider may charge separately. These costs are not always the same as OKX trading fees.

### Withdrawal fee

If you later move BTC to an external wallet, a withdrawal fee may apply. The amount can depend on the network and current platform conditions. Check the withdrawal screen before sending.

### Network fee

Bitcoin transactions use the Bitcoin network, which can become more expensive or slower when demand increases. This is separate from the price you paid for the asset.

A useful comparison is:

> **Total effective cost = money paid minus the market value of the BTC received**

For a small purchase, a modest fixed payment fee can have a noticeable effect. For a larger order, spread and execution price may matter more.
